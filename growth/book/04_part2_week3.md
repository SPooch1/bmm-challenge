# Week 3: Activation

# Day 15: Your Next Move, Part 1: Line Them Up

For the next three days, you'll take your biggest opportunity and make it easier to repeat. I call these three days **Your Next Move**: a slightly bigger step, built on the small habits you already have.

Part 1 is alignment. Before you push on any one pillar, you want to make sure the three aren't fighting each other.

Here's what fighting looks like:

- You're trying to save money, but your stress recovery is shopping. Every rough day costs you $40.
- You're trying to walk more, but you're sleeping five hours a night and too tired to move.
- You're trying to sleep better, but you lie awake worrying about money.

In each case, one pillar is pulling against another. You can push as hard as you want on the first one. The second one will keep pulling it back.

Look at your three pillars and your notes from the last two weeks. **Find one place where two of them are working against each other.** Then write down one small fix for that connection.

Examples:

- *Stress and money fighting:* "When I'm stressed, I'll take a 10-minute walk instead of opening a shopping app." (That's a body win too.)
- *Body and sleep fighting:* "Phone charges in the kitchen, not by the bed."
- *Money and stress fighting:* "I'll check my balance on Friday mornings instead of avoiding it. No surprises, less dread."

Notice that some of these fixes touch two pillars at once.

**Today:** Find one place where two pillars are working against each other. Write down one small fix, and start it today.

**Hard day version:** Just name the two pillars that fight the most.

# Day 16: Your Next Move, Part 2: Your 15 Percent Moves

On Day 11, you circled the biggest opportunity for about a 15 percent improvement in each pillar. Today, you turn each one into a specific move.

For each pillar, write one move you can start right now:

- **Money:** one spending leak or comfort expense to trim by about 15 percent, with the savings going straight to your Peace of Mind Account.
- **Body:** one movement or eating adjustment about 15 percent bigger than your current baseline.
- **Stress:** one change in how you respond to your most common trigger.

Make each one pass three tests:

1. **Specific.** Could someone else tell whether you did it today? "Spend less" fails. "Pack lunch Tuesday through Thursday" passes.
2. **Small enough for a bad week.** If it only works when everything goes right, it will stop the first time things go wrong.
3. **Connected to a number.** It should move something you're already tracking.

Some real examples:

- "Cut takeout from four nights to three. Move $45 a month to Peace of Mind, automatically."
- "Walk 15 minutes at lunch on workdays."
- "Four rounds of 4x4x4x4 breathing in the car before I walk into work."

Now, following the book's own rule of one at a time: **choose one of the three to start today.** Pick the one that also helps another pillar, or the one you're most likely to keep. Save the other two for after Day 21, when the first one feels ordinary.

## Common snag: "I can't decide which move to pick"

Pick the one that also helps another pillar, since you're starting just one for now. A lunchtime walk helps your body and your stress. Packing lunch helps your money and often your body. When one move helps two pillars, you get two wins for the effort of one.

**Today:** Write down your three 15 percent moves, one per pillar. Choose one and start it today.

**Hard day version:** Write down one move and start it.

# Day 17: Your Next Move, Part 3: Lock It In

Here's where most plans fall apart. You've picked good moves. You're motivated. And motivation is exactly what won't last.

So today, you take the move you started yesterday and answer one question: **how will this happen without me having to want it?**

Don't make the action depend on feeling motivated. Decide what will remind you, remove a step, or make the action easier to begin on an ordinary day.

Here's how that looks in each pillar:

**Money: automate it.** Set up an automatic transfer from checking to your Peace of Mind Account on payday. Even a small recurring amount builds over time. If splitting direct deposit fits your situation and your employer allows it, that's another way to automate it.

**Body: set up the environment.** Pack your walking shoes in the car. Put the fruit on the counter and the chips on a high shelf (or don't buy them). Lay out tomorrow's workout clothes tonight. Fill a water bottle before bed and leave it by the coffee maker.

**Stress: set a cue.** Set a phone reminder or an hourly watch chime. Put a sticky note on your monitor with "4x4" on it. Decide that every time you sit down in your car, you'll do four rounds before you start the engine.

For your move, write down the automation, the environment change, or the cue that will make it happen. Then set it up. Today, not tomorrow. Setting up the transfer takes five minutes. Doing it "later" is how it doesn't happen.

**Today:** For the move you started, write down and set up one thing that makes it happen without relying on motivation.

**Hard day version:** Set up one thing now that makes tomorrow's move easier: a $5 transfer, or your shoes by the door.

# Day 18: Hook It to Something You Already Do

You already have routines that happen every day without fail. You make coffee. You brush your teeth. You sit down at your desk. You eat lunch. You get in the car. You get into bed.

Those routines are some of the most reliable things in your life. So today, you're going to use one of them as the reminder for your new move.

Pick one routine that already happens every day, at a time that makes sense for the move you started on Day 16. Then decide on the one small action you'll do right at that point.

A few examples of how this can look:

- The coffee is brewing. That's when you do four rounds of breathing.
- You've just sat down at your desk. That's when you fill your water bottle.
- You've parked at work. That's your cue to take the stairs.
- Payday arrives. That's the day you check that your Peace of Mind transfer went through.
- You've brushed your teeth at night. The phone goes on the kitchen charger.

Keep the action under two minutes. The routine does the remembering. The new action just rides along.

If your one simple action from Day 9 doesn't have a reliable moment yet, you can give it one too. But don't add anything brand new today. Two anchored actions are plenty.

## Common snag: "My days are all different"

Then anchor to things that happen every day no matter what: waking up, the first cup of coffee, brushing your teeth, getting into bed. Even the most chaotic schedule has a few fixed points.

**Today:** Choose one daily routine and attach your new move to it. Write down the routine and the action.

**Hard day version:** Attach one round of breathing to your morning coffee (or whatever you do first every day).

# Day 19: When You Slip

Plan on missing a day eventually. The useful question is what you do next.

You'll miss a day. You'll dip into your Peace of Mind Account for something that wasn't really a storm. You'll eat the whole sleeve of cookies. You'll have a week where the breath never happens.

That's not failure. That's information.

Here's something encouraging from the research on how habits form. In a well-known 2010 study from University College London, people picked a new daily behavior and tracked how automatic it felt over 12 weeks. **Missing a single day had little effect on how the habit developed.** What matters is getting back to it, not keeping a perfect streak.

So the goal isn't to never slip. The goal is to have a plan for when you do: a **reset plan**.

## Writing your reset plan

Write one short paragraph that answers these questions:

1. **What's my smallest restart?** The "hard day version" of your habit, the thing so easy you can always do it. One round of breathing. A two-minute walk. Moving $5 to savings.
2. **When do I restart?** The answer is always: the very next chance. Not Monday. Not next month. Not January 1st. Next chance.
3. **What do I ask?** Not "what's wrong with me?" but "what got in the way?" Was it the schedule? The environment? Was the habit too big? Then adjust the system, not your opinion of yourself.
4. **If I used my Peace of Mind money:** was it a real storm? Then the account did what it was for. Now start refilling it with your next transfer.

Here's an example:

*"When I slip, I'll do the smallest version the very next day: one round of breathing, a walk around the block, and $5 to Peace of Mind. I'll ask what got in the way and fix that. No starting over, no waiting for Monday. If I use my Peace of Mind money for a real storm, I'll bump my transfer by $10 a week until it's back."*

Put your reset plan somewhere you'll find it when you need it. The back of your notebook. A note on your phone called "When I slip."

**Today:** Write your reset plan in one paragraph.

**Hard day version:** Write just your smallest restart for each pillar.

# Day 20: Your One Page

Today you pull everything together onto one page: your plan for the next 90 days.

Take a single sheet of paper (or one note on your phone) and fill in the blanks:

- My automatic transfer is: $______ every ______

**Body**
- My daily movement baseline is: ______

**Stress**
- My most common trigger is: ______

**Anchors**
- When ______ happens, I ______.

That's it. One page. Everything that matters, nothing that doesn't.

Look at what's on it. Three weeks ago, you probably couldn't have filled in half these blanks. Now you can fill in every one.

Put this page somewhere you'll see it: on the fridge, in your wallet, or as a photo on your phone. Read it once a week. It's your baseline, written down.

## Common snag: "Some of my blanks are still empty"

That's fine. Fill in what you know. An honest one page with a few blanks is more useful than a complete one full of guesses. Come back to the blanks during your weekly check. They'll fill in as you go.

**Today:** Fill in your one page.

**Hard day version:** Fill in just the three "My ... is" lines you know best.

# Day 21: What Comes Next

You made it. Twenty-one days.

Take a minute and look back at Day 0, when you wrote one sentence about where you were in money, body, and stress. Read those three sentences now.

Then write three new ones. Where are you *today*?

The difference may be small. That's fine. Small enough to continue is more useful here than big enough to exhaust you. You've practiced a different kind of change: smaller actions built around ordinary days.

## Your next 90 days

Twenty-one days built the foundation. The next 90 are where it sets. Here's a simple plan (Part Three has more on each piece):

**Days 22 to 30: Keep your margin.** Don't add anything new. Just keep the habits on your one page.

**Days 31 to 60: Add one thing.** Once your habits feel ordinary, start one of the other 15 percent moves you designed on Day 16. Just one.

**Days 61 to 90: Keep going.** Keep your one page, add one more move if you're ready, and keep doing your weekly check. Pushing hard on one goal comes later, once the floor is solid.

Put a reminder on your calendar for 30, 60, and 90 days from today. At each one, reread your one page and update your numbers.

<b>You already know how habits work. This is for the part after the reading: the days where you actually do something.</b>
<p>Diets snap back. Budgets snap back. The big fresh start that felt so good in January can be gone a few months later. It is easy to blame the plan, or yourself. The pull comes from somewhere else: your baseline, the result your ordinary days tend to produce. A plan is a push. When the push ends, the ordinary days are still there.</p>
<p>Bend Your Baseline is a short book with one job: raise the floor you fall back to, with changes small enough to keep after the push is over.</p>
<p><b>Part One</b> explains why good intentions revert, and why more information, more motivation, more personalization, and more apps do not fix it. Four short chapters you can read in one sitting.</p>
<p><b>Part Two</b> is 21 days, one short chapter per day, each ending with something to do today in less time than it takes to read the chapter. You will find your Peace of Mind number, start a Peace of Mind Account, set a body baseline you can hold on your worst week, and build a stress blueprint from what actually happens in your days. Every day has a hard-day version, small enough to do when everything goes wrong.</p>
<p><b>Part Three</b> is Day 22 and after: how to keep what you built, what to do when the storm hits, and a bonus chapter for anyone who leads a team and has watched a wellness program or company initiative fade.</p>
<p>A free companion app follows the book day by day, with a breathing timer and a savings tracker. Nothing to download from an app store. Paper works fine too; the trackers are in the back.</p>
<p>Scott Pooch is a retirement plan consultant and educator in Columbus, Ohio, and a former college distance running coach. He has spent more than twenty years watching employers spend real money on programs their people stop using. This book is what he tells the people in the room.</p>
<p>Start smaller than you think you need to. If you cannot do it on your worst day, it is too big. Shrink it.</p>

# Week 2: Momentum

# Day 8: Where the Money Goes

A lot of us spend money on autopilot. Today, we switch the autopilot off for about thirty minutes.

Pull up your last 30 days of spending. Your bank statement, your credit card statement, or both. Go line by line and sort each charge into one of three piles:

**Essentials.** The things you need: housing, groceries, utilities, getting to work, insurance, minimum payments.

**Comfort.** The small pleasures that make life better: coffee out, a streaming service you actually watch, dinner with friends. There is nothing wrong with this pile. You're not going to cut it to zero.

**Leaks.** Money that leaves without you noticing or enjoying it. The subscription you forgot about. The app you tried once. The late fee. The membership you keep meaning to use. The "free trial" that wasn't.

Add up each pile.

Leaks are a good place to find money for your Peace of Mind Account, because plugging them doesn't make your life any worse. You weren't using them anyway.

Before you put the list away, look at it one more time and try a version of what author James Clear calls the alien test: *if someone saw only this list, and couldn't hear any of my explanations, what would they say my priorities are?* Your spending can show priorities your intentions don't. If the answer surprises you, that's not a reason for guilt. It's a clue about where to point your next small move.

## Moving toward your first marker

For the rest of the 21 days, your job is to move money toward your first marker. How much depends on your situation, and any amount counts. This is the one place in the book where I'll suggest pushing a little harder for a short time, if you can. Some places to look:

- **Plug the leaks** you just found.
- **Sell something** you're not using. Electronics, tools, sporting equipment, furniture. Online marketplaces make this easy.
- **Delay a purchase** or two you were planning. Not cancel. Delay. Move that money to the account instead.
- **Redirect a windfall.** A tax refund, a bonus, a cash gift, a rebate.
- **Direct some extra earnings here**, if picking up extra work is realistic for you.

This short push is not your long-term plan. It's a quick burst to build a foundation. Once you reach your first marker, you'll switch to small, automatic amounts you barely notice.

## Common snag: "I keep spending the money I save"

That's why the account should be separate, and ideally at a different bank. Make it slightly inconvenient to get to. Don't link a debit card to it. Hide it from your main screen if your bank app allows it. That small bit of friction is enough to make you think twice. And when you do need it for a real storm, that's what it's for.

## Common snag: "I feel guilty about the Comfort pile"

Don't. A life with no small pleasures is exactly the high-intensity plan that snaps back. The goal isn't to live on rice and beans. It's to know where the money goes, plug the leaks, and choose the comforts on purpose instead of on autopilot.

**Today:** Sort your last 30 days of spending into Essentials, Comfort, and Leaks. Pick one leak to plug this week, and move that amount into your Peace of Mind Account. Then give your list the alien test.

**Hard day version:** Find one subscription you don't use and cancel it.

# Day 9: Just a Little More

Look at your movement numbers from last week. What was your daily average?

Today, add 5 to 10 percent.

If you averaged 4,000 steps, aim for 4,200 to 4,400. If you moved on purpose for 10 minutes a day, aim for 11. That's it.

It sounds almost too small to bother with. That's the point. A dramatic change can leave you sore, tired, hungry, or unable to fit the plan into your day. A small increase is easier to fit. Choose one that's appropriate for you.

A few places to find the extra:

- Park farther away.
- Take the stairs for one flight.
- Walk while you're on a phone call.
- Take the long way to the bathroom at work.
- Walk around the block before you go inside at the end of the day.

## Pick your one simple action

Today is also the day to choose your **one simple action** for your health, if you haven't already. You started thinking about it on Day 3. Now pick.

Write it down, and make it specific. Not "eat better." Instead:

- "No eating after 7 p.m."
- "A glass of water before every meal."
- "Only fruit or nuts for afternoon snacks, and I'll keep them in my bag."

Then ask yourself one question: *Could I do this during the worst week of my year?* If the answer is no, make it smaller.

## Common snag: "5 percent is too small to matter"

On any single day, you're right. But you're not doing this for a single day. Keeping it small gives the action a better chance of fitting your life after the novelty wears off. You can always add another 5 percent next month. What you can't do is keep a pace your life won't support.

**Today:** Add 5 to 10 percent to your daily movement. Write down your one simple action and start it today.

**Hard day version:** Walk for two extra minutes. That counts.

# Day 10: Stress Is Data

Instead of fighting your stress response, today we're going to learn from it.

Your stress response isn't broken. It's information: a dashboard light, not a fire alarm. When the light comes on, the useful question isn't "how do I make this stop?" It's "what is this telling me?"

Look at your stress blueprint from Week 1. Pick **one** trigger that showed up more than once, or the one that felt the worst.

Write down three things:

1. **The trigger.** Be specific. Not "work." Instead: "the Monday 9 a.m. meeting," or "opening my credit card app."
2. **Your body's response.** Where did you feel it, and how?
3. **What you needed in that moment.** This is the new part. Were you hungry? Tired? Unprepared? Did you need information you didn't have? Did you need five minutes alone? Did you need someone to say it would be okay?

That third question can change things. Sometimes a stress trigger points to something practical you need: preparation, information, rest, food, time, or help. The Monday meeting might be stressful because you walk in unprepared, which is fixable with 15 minutes on Friday afternoon. Opening the credit card app might be stressful because you don't know what you'll see, and the clearer picture from Day 8 may help with that.

Not every trigger has a fix. Some just need a better recovery. That's what the breath is for.

## Common snag: "My stress is too big for this"

Sometimes it is. If your stress feels overwhelming most days, or it's affecting your sleep, your relationships, or your health, the breath is a good tool but not a complete answer. Please talk with a doctor, a counselor, or your employer's assistance program if you have one. Asking for help is a sign that you're taking your baseline seriously.

**Today:** Pick one trigger. Write the trigger, your body's response, and what you needed. Do the 4x4x4x4 breath at least once.

**Hard day version:** Do the breath once. Name the trigger in one word.

# Day 11: Find Your Biggest Opportunity

In four days, you'll start three days I call **Your Next Move**: you take your biggest opportunity and make it easier to repeat. Today, you get ready.

Go back to your three-pillar snapshot from Day 5. Read it again with everything you've learned since.

Now ask: **in each pillar, where's the biggest opportunity for about a 15 percent improvement?**

Why 15 percent? No magic. It means noticeable, but not an overhaul. More than the 5 percent nudge from Day 9, nowhere near a "change everything" plan. Adjust it down if it's too much for your situation.

Some examples:

- *Money:* "I spend about $300 a month on takeout. Cutting 15 percent is about $45. That goes straight to my Peace of Mind Account."
- *Body:* "My sleep averages 5 out of 10. If I put my phone in the kitchen at 10 p.m., I think I could get to 6."
- *Stress:* "My Monday meeting ruins the whole morning. If I prep for 15 minutes on Friday, I could walk in calmer."

Don't commit to anything yet. Just circle the opportunity in each pillar. Think about it for a few days.

**Today:** Reread your Day 5 snapshot. Circle the biggest opportunity for a 15 percent improvement in each pillar.

**Hard day version:** Circle one opportunity in one pillar.

# Day 12: Keep Score, Simply

You don't need a fancy system to track your progress. A simple one you keep beats a fancy one you stop opening.

Today, set up a tracker for the rest of the challenge. Three columns: **Money**, **Body**, **Stress**. One row per day.

What goes in each column is up to you, but keep it to one quick mark or number:

- **Money:** How much you added to your Peace of Mind Account today (even $0 is a number), or your running balance.
- **Body:** Did you do your one simple action? A check mark. Or your step count.
- **Stress:** How many times you did the breath, or your end-of-day stress level from 1 to 10.

Use whatever you already use. A notebook. A note on your phone. A paper calendar on the fridge with three colored pens. (There's a tracker you can copy at the back of this book, and the app has one built in.)

The rule: **tracking should take under a minute a day.** If it takes longer, it will be the first thing you drop. Then you'll drop the thing it was tracking.

One more tip: track *actions*, not just *results*. Your weight or your balance won't move every day. But you can check off the action every day, and that's the part you control.

## Common snag: "I've tried tracking apps before and quit"

If you've quit tracking apps before, complexity may have been part of it. Many ask for a lot: calories, macros, categories, streaks, badges. This tracker asks for three marks a day. If even that feels like too much, track just one pillar, the one you care about most, and add the others later.

**Today:** Set up your three-column tracker and fill in today's row.

**Hard day version:** Put a check mark on today's date on a calendar if you did your one simple action.

# Day 13: What You Wish You'd Known

You're almost halfway through.

Today's question is short: **what's one thing you wish you'd had clarity on earlier?**

Maybe it's how much you really need each month. Maybe it's how little you were actually moving. Maybe it's what triggers your stress, or how much better you feel after a few rounds of breathing. Maybe it's how many subscriptions you were paying for.

Write it down.

The thing you wish you'd known earlier was invisible to you. It was in your baseline, running quietly in the background. You couldn't fix it because you couldn't see it.

That thing is worth building a system around. If you wish you'd known your spending sooner, set up a five-minute weekly money check. If you wish you'd known how little you moved, keep a step count on your phone's home screen. If you wish you'd noticed your tension sooner, set an hourly reminder.

I have my watch set to chime every hour for exactly this reason. Without a reminder, I'd get to the end of the day and realize I'd been stressed the whole time. The chime is my cue to stop, drop my shoulders, and take a couple of breaths.

**Today:** Write down the one thing you wish you'd had clarity on earlier. Then write one simple way to keep it visible from now on.

**Hard day version:** Just write the one thing.

# Day 14: Halfway

You're halfway through.

In two weeks, you've mapped your baseline in three areas of your life. You know your Peace of Mind number. You know how much you move and how you sleep. You know what sets off your stress and where you feel it. You've made a few small changes, and you're tracking them.

You know things now that you didn't know on Day 0.

Today, write down **three specific changes** you've noticed since Day 1. Big or small: an action, a number, or just something you noticed. Some made-up examples:

- "I have $175 in my Peace of Mind Account. I've never had a separate savings account before."
- "I'm walking about 400 more steps a day and I don't really notice."
- "I did the breath before my Monday meeting and it went better."
- "I canceled two subscriptions I forgot I had."
- "I noticed I've stopped clenching my jaw in traffic. Mostly."

Then write down **one thing you want to nail in the final seven days**. Just one.

Missed a few days? Same exercise. Write down what you *have* noticed, then pick up at the next day. There's no penalty for a missed day. The only way to fail this is to decide you've failed.

**Today:** Write three changes you've noticed since Day 1, and the one thing you want to nail in the last week.

**Hard day version:** Write one change.

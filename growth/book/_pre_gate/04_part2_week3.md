# Week 3: Activation

# Day 15: Turbocharge, Part 1: Line Them Up

Welcome to the **Turbocharge Protocol**. For the next three days, you'll take your biggest opportunity in each pillar and build it into your life so it doesn't depend on willpower.

Part 1 is alignment. Before you push on any one pillar, you want to make sure the three aren't fighting each other.

Here's what fighting looks like:

- You're trying to save money, but your stress recovery is shopping. Every rough day costs you $40.
- You're trying to walk more, but you're sleeping five hours a night and too tired to move.
- You're trying to sleep better, but you lie awake worrying about money.

In each case, one pillar is pulling against another. You can push as hard as you want on the first one. The second one will keep pulling it back.

Look at your three pillars and your notes from the last two weeks. **Find one place where two of them are working against each other.** Then write down one small fix for that connection.

Examples:

- *Stress and money fighting:* "When I'm stressed, I'll take a 10-minute walk instead of opening a shopping app." (That's a body win too.)
- *Body and sleep fighting:* "Phone charges in the kitchen, not by the bed."
- *Money and stress fighting:* "I'll check my balance on Friday mornings instead of avoiding it. No surprises, less dread."

Notice how many of these fixes help two pillars at once. That's the good version of the loop.

**Today:** Find one place where two pillars are working against each other. Write down one small fix, and start it today.

**Hard day version:** Just name the two pillars that fight the most.

# Day 16: Turbocharge, Part 2: Your 15 Percent Moves

On Day 11, you circled the biggest opportunity for about a 15 percent improvement in each pillar. Today, you turn each one into a specific move.

For each pillar, write one move you can start right now:

- **Money:** one spending leak or comfort expense to trim by about 15 percent, with the savings going straight to your Peace of Mind Account.
- **Body:** one movement or eating adjustment about 15 percent bigger than your current baseline.
- **Stress:** one change in how you respond to your most common trigger.

Make each one pass three tests:

1. **Specific.** Could someone else tell whether you did it today? "Spend less" fails. "Pack lunch Tuesday through Thursday" passes.
2. **Small enough for a bad week.** If it only works when everything goes right, it will stop the first time things go wrong.
3. **Connected to a number.** It should move something you're already tracking.

Some real examples:

- "Cut takeout from four nights to three. Move $45 a month to Peace of Mind, automatically."
- "Walk 15 minutes at lunch on workdays."
- "Four rounds of 4x4x4x4 breathing in the car before I walk into work."

Three moves. That's all. You're not overhauling your life. You're making three changes big enough to feel and small enough to keep.

## Common snag: "I can't decide which move to pick"

Pick the one that also helps another pillar. A lunchtime walk helps your body and your stress. Packing lunch helps your money and often your body. When one move helps two pillars, you get two wins for the effort of one.

**Today:** Write down your three 15 percent moves, one per pillar. Start at least one of them today.

**Hard day version:** Write down one move and start it.

# Day 17: Turbocharge, Part 3: Lock It In

Here's where most plans fall apart. You've picked good moves. You're motivated. And motivation is exactly what won't last.

So today, you take each of your three moves and answer one question: **how will this happen without me having to want it?**

**Design beats willpower.** Every time. When you're tired, stressed, and running late, you won't rise to the occasion. You'll fall back to whatever's easiest. So make the right thing the easiest thing.

Here's how that looks in each pillar:

**Money: automate it.** Set up an automatic transfer from checking to your Peace of Mind Account on payday. Even $12.50 a week adds up to $650 a year. If your employer offers direct deposit to more than one account, have a small slice of every paycheck go straight to Peace of Mind. You'll never see it, so you'll never miss it.

**Body: set up the environment.** Pack your walking shoes in the car. Put the fruit on the counter and the chips on a high shelf (or don't buy them). Lay out tomorrow's workout clothes tonight. Fill a water bottle before bed and leave it by the coffee maker.

**Stress: set a cue.** Set a phone reminder or an hourly watch chime. Put a sticky note on your monitor with "4x4" on it. Decide that every time you sit down in your car, you'll do four rounds before you start the engine.

For each of your three moves, write down the automation, the environment change, or the cue that will make it happen. Then set it up. Today, not tomorrow. Setting up the transfer takes five minutes. Doing it "later" is how it doesn't happen.

**Today:** For each of your three moves, write down and set up one thing that makes it happen automatically.

**Hard day version:** Set up the automatic savings transfer. Even $5 a week.

# Day 18: Hook It to Something You Already Do

You already have routines that happen every day without fail. You make coffee. You brush your teeth. You sit down at your desk. You eat lunch. You get in the car. You get into bed.

Those routines are the most reliable things in your life. So today, you're going to hook new habits onto them.

You may have seen this idea called "habit stacking." It's simple: pick a routine that already happens, and attach one small new action to it. The old routine becomes the reminder for the new one.

The formula: **After I [existing routine], I will [new habit].**

Examples:

- "After I pour my morning coffee, I'll do four rounds of 4x4x4x4 breathing while it cools."
- "After I sit down at my desk, I'll fill my water bottle."
- "After I park at work, I'll take the stairs."
- "After I get paid, I'll check that my Peace of Mind transfer went through."
- "After I brush my teeth at night, I'll put my phone on the kitchen charger."

Identify **three** routines you do every day: one in the morning, one in the middle of the day, one in the evening. For each, write down one new habit you'll hook onto it.

Pick habits that take under two minutes. The routine carries the weight. The new habit just rides along.

## Common snag: "My days are all different"

Then hook your new habits to things that happen every day no matter what: waking up, the first cup of coffee, brushing your teeth, getting into bed. Even the most chaotic schedule has a few fixed points. Those are your hooks.

**Today:** Write down three existing routines and one new habit hooked onto each.

**Hard day version:** Hook one habit onto your morning coffee (or whatever you do first every day).

# Day 19: When You Slip

You're going to slip. Everybody does.

You'll miss a day. You'll dip into your Peace of Mind Account for something that wasn't really a storm. You'll eat the whole sleeve of cookies. You'll have a week where the breath never happens.

That's not failure. That's information.

Here's something encouraging from the research on how habits form. In a well-known 2010 study from University College London, people picked a new daily behavior and tracked how automatic it felt over 12 weeks. **Missing a single day had little effect on how the habit developed.** What matters is getting back to it, not keeping a perfect streak.

So the goal isn't to never slip. The goal is to have a plan for when you do. I call it the **Reset Protocol**.

## Writing your Reset Protocol

Write one short paragraph that answers these questions:

1. **What's my smallest restart?** The "hard day version" of your habit, the thing so easy you can always do it. One round of breathing. A two-minute walk. Moving $5 to savings.
2. **When do I restart?** The answer is always: the very next chance. Not Monday. Not next month. Not January 1st. Next chance.
3. **What do I ask?** Not "what's wrong with me?" but "what got in the way?" Was it the schedule? The environment? Was the habit too big? Then adjust the system, not your opinion of yourself.
4. **If I used my Peace of Mind money:** that's what it's for. Was it a real storm? Good. The account did its job. Now refill it, starting with your next transfer.

Here's an example:

*"When I slip, I'll do the smallest version the very next day: one round of breathing, a walk around the block, and $5 to Peace of Mind. I'll ask what got in the way and fix that. No starting over, no waiting for Monday. If I use my Peace of Mind money for a real storm, I'll bump my transfer by $10 a week until it's back."*

Put your Reset Protocol somewhere you'll find it when you need it. The back of your notebook. A note on your phone called "When I slip."

**Today:** Write your Reset Protocol in one paragraph.

**Hard day version:** Write just your smallest restart for each pillar.

# Day 21: What Comes Next

You made it. Twenty-one days.

Take a minute and look back at Day 0, when you wrote one sentence about where you were in money, body, and stress. Read those three sentences now.

Then write three new ones. Where are you *today*?

The difference may be small. That's fine. Small and permanent beats big and temporary every time. You haven't finished anything. You've started the only kind of change that tends to stay.

## Your next 90 days

Twenty-one days built the foundation. The next 90 are where it sets. Here's a simple plan (Part Three has more on each piece):

**Days 22 to 30: Keep your margin.** Don't add anything new. Just keep the habits on your one page. This is the stretch where most programs die, because the novelty wears off. Your job is to be boring and consistent.

**Days 31 to 60: Add one thing.** Once your habits feel automatic, add one more small action in whichever pillar needs it most. Just one.

**Days 61 to 90: Your first sprint (optional).** If you're ready, pick one pillar and run a focused 60-to-90-day burst with specificity, recovery, and rest built in. Chapter 6 shows you how.

Put a reminder on your calendar for 30, 60, and 90 days from today. At each one, reread your one page and update your numbers.

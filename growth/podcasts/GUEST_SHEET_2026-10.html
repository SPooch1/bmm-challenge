Short bio

Scott works with business owners and their employees on the everyday habits behind money, health, and stress.

He is a retirement plan consultant and educator based in Columbus, Ohio, and the founder of 401Grow, with more than 20 years working with business owners. He speaks to conferences, associations, and companies on why good intentions snap back. His keynote, Bend Your Baseline, is the source of his book.

Before any of that, he was a runner. He ran the 800 meters in college and later coached college distance runners for four years, which is where he first learned that the easy days matter as much as the hard ones. He turns 60 this year, so some years the goal is simply to keep his own baseline from sliding.

The idea in one line: a program is a push with an end date. When the push ends, you go back to your baseline, so the work is raising the baseline itself, one small action at a time.

Topics he can speak to

  • Living on the yo-yo. Why diets, budgets and fresh starts feel great for a few weeks, then snap back, and why that is a design problem, not a character problem.
  • Your baseline. The resting state you return to when you are not trying, and the three worth watching: money, body, stress.
  • Expense or emergency. The Peace of Mind Account, a separate account for the next surprise bill, and the first $500 marker.
  • Day 22 is the most dangerous day. Keeping your margin after a 21-day start: the weekly check, the hard day version, the reset plan.
  • For people who lead others. Why workplace wellness and financial programs fade after launch, the four things programs lean on, and a two-minute check for any initiative.
Ground rules he keeps on air. Education, not advice. No outcome claims: the 21-day program is new and he does not quote results. He credits borrowed ideas by name.

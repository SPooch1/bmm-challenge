The Story

It started with a book in a library

Brian Davenport was a rock guitarist first. Then he spotted a copy of The Handmade Music Factory on the way into a library, and that was more or less that.

No apprenticeship, no shop class — just cigar boxes, scrap wood, a pile of hardware, and a lot of learning on the job. Dozens of builds later, the Hot Sauce Guitar Kitchen runs out of a home shop in Columbus, Ohio, turning out three- and four-string instruments that no one else will ever have a copy of.

The name? He's a hot sauce guy. Every build ships with a glass slide, because the blues players are going to want one anyway.

As featured on Spectrum News 1, Columbus.

In the Kitchen

How a box becomes an instrument

  1. 01

    Find the box

    Cigar shops, estate sales, and boxes people mail in because their grandfather smoked the cigars. The box picks the guitar as much as the other way around.

  2. 02

    Cut the neck

    Salvaged and shop-stock hardwood, planed and shaped by hand, then fretted properly — these are meant to be played in tune, not just looked at.

  3. 03

    Wire it up

    A humbucker, a working volume and tone, an intonatable bridge. The electronics are real; nothing here is decoration.

  4. 04

    Play it

    Strung, set up, and played before it goes anywhere. The best part of the job is hearing the thing speak for the first time.

Questions

The things people ask

Do I need to know how to play guitar?

No. Three-string cigar box guitars are one of the friendliest instruments there is — tuned to an open chord, one finger across the strings gets you a chord that works. Plenty of owners started here.

Three strings or four?

Three is the traditional cigar box setup and the easiest place to start, especially for slide. Four gives you more range if you're already a guitar player. If you're torn, say so on the form and you'll get a straight recommendation.

Can I send in my own cigar box?

Yes, and it's some of the best work that comes out of the shop — boxes with a story attached. Mail it in and the guitar gets built around it, label and all.

Are these actually playable, or wall art?

Playable. Fretted necks, intonatable bridges, humbucker pickups with working volume and tone. Every one is strung, set up and played before it ships.

What's the glass slide about?

It's a hot sauce shop — the slides come from bottles, and every build ships with one. If you play blues, you'll use it.

How long does a commission take?

It depends what's already on the bench. You'll get a real timeline with your quote rather than a guess up front.
